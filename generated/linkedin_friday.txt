What if you could get AI to follow your instructions perfectly—no coding required?
Here are 3 prompt‑engineering tricks anyone can use.

#PromptEngineering

⚠️ These tips work best with current LLMs; results may vary across models.

When I first started experimenting with AI chatbots, I treated them like a search engine—typing short keywords and hoping for magic. The answers were vague, off‑topic, or just plain wrong. I realized the model wasn’t guessing my intent; it was waiting for clear direction.

That shift in mindset was the first prompt‑engineering lesson: give the AI a specific role and context. Instead of “Tell me about marketing,” I began with “You are a senior marketing strategist with 10 years of experience in B2B SaaS. Outline a three‑month launch plan for a new analytics tool.” The responses became detailed, relevant, and surprisingly creative.

I also discovered the power of iterative refinement. My first prompt rarely delivered exactly what I needed. But by asking follow-up questions, providing feedback like "make it more concise" or "focus on the target audience in Europe," I could guide the AI to a much better outcome. It's less about getting it right the first time and more about being a good editor. Think of it as a conversation, not a command.
